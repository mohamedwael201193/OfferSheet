The money math

One formula. Nothing hidden.

The verdict is arithmetic, not a prediction. The formula is on the page, with a checker you can run yourself in the app.

Guarantee $250,000 ÷ Ticket $120 = 2,084 tickets

$250,000 ÷ $120 = 2,083.33 — rounded up: 2,084

Optional venue, production, marketing, and other fixed costs are added only when entered, and an entered sponsor offset is subtracted. A blank field is not entered. The recorded trials entered none of these fields, so the count above stays 2,084 tickets.

If break-even exceeds a known capacity, the verdict is “Cannot break even in this room at this guarantee.” If Ticketmaster returns a room record with no capacity field, the page says: “Venue capacity unknown — Ticketmaster returned no capacity for this room. Break-even is shown as tickets needed, not as a sellout percentage. Verify room size before signing.” Other unknown causes render their own sentences (key not configured, or Ticketmaster unavailable). Never a made-up number, never a sellout percentage on unknown ground.

Illustrative arithmetic on the recorded trial. Being wrong by 100 tickets = $12,000 of guarantee exposure (100 × 120 = 12,000). That 100-ticket gap is hypothetical — not a measured outcome, not a real loss, and not a prediction.

Why the phone shows a record, not a live verdict

A picture of a verdict looks like a verdict, and this page refuses to show one that nobody ran. So the phone shows a clearly labelled record of a trial that did run — 29 Oct 2026 — and nothing else. For a live verdict on your own offer, use “Try the live demo” or the Android app.
